Habitica has been gamifying the to-do list for more than a decade, and it's still the first app people recommend for turning chores into a quest. But there's a gap between what Habitica does well (habits, dailies, life admin with an RPG skin) and what training needs. A workout isn't a checkbox: it's sets, reps, rest, progressive overload, and a history you can learn from. If your training outgrew its loop, here are the strongest fitness-first options, what they cost, and how to move your habit-RPG workflow over.

## What Habitica does well

Habitica's core is free. You create an avatar and track three task types: Habits (repeated actions that can give or cost points), Dailies (recurring tasks), and To-Dos (one-off tasks). Completing them earns XP and gold; missing Dailies drains HP, and at zero HP you lose a level and gear. At level 10 you pick a class (Warrior, Mage, Rogue, or Healer), and you can join parties and guilds for story quests where the group defeats a boss with real-life task completion.

It runs on the web, iOS, Android, and Wear OS with full syncing. The optional subscription ($4.99/month or $47.99/year, plus group plans from $9/month) adds monthly gems and cosmetics, not mechanics; the developers are explicit that it isn't pay-to-win. That's why it survived: skipped habits hurt, and quests make teammates rely on you.

## Where Habitica falls short for fitness

The limit shows up when training is the goal. Habitica tracks completion, not performance. There's no exercise library, no set/rep/weight logging, no rest timer, no volume or PR tracking, no progressive-overload guidance. A 20-minute walk and a heavy squat session check the same box; you can't review last month's volume or get told to add weight.

For general habits (water, sleep, stretching) it's plenty. For real progress, you want a game layer on top of workout data, not a to-do list. Unsure whether gamification is even the right lever? Our [best gamified fitness apps](/best-gamified-fitness-apps) roundup covers the whole field.

## The alternatives, app by app

### Workout Quest

Workout Quest is Habitica's closest structural cousin aimed at training: every set earns XP, levels unlock quests, streaks keep the loop alive, and an AI coach builds sessions around your level and equipment. The game layer sits on real workout data: an exercise library, sets and reps, rest timers, one-rep-max and volume tracking, a muscle heat map, leaderboards, and guild-style quests. It's free to start, and everything works offline. Want the RPG feeling without losing progressive overload? This is the move; our guide to [turning lifts into quests](/gamify-your-progress-workout-quest-quests) explains how the quest system maps onto a real training week.

### Zombies, Run!

Zombies, Run! gamifies cardio instead of strength: you're a runner in a zombie apocalypse whose real pace drives the story as you outrun hordes, collect supplies, and rebuild your base across 600+ audio missions. Free to download with episodes unlocking weekly, then a subscription for the full library (around $6.99/month or $49.99/year). The best narrative running game around, it gamifies only one side of fitness; see our [Zombies, Run! alternatives](/zombies-run-alternatives) for runners who've outgrown it.

### Forest

Forest isn't a fitness app; it's a focus timer that plants a virtual tree for every distraction-free session, and leave the app early and the tree dies. One-time ~$3.99 (region-dependent) on iOS, Android, and browser. It earns a spot here for one job: protecting the 45 minutes you set aside to train.

### Streaks

Streaks is the minimalist end of the spectrum: up to 24 tasks, complete them daily, keep the chain alive. One-time ~$4.99 (about $5.99 in some regions), Apple-only, with Apple Health integration and iCloud sync. No XP, no avatars, no quests; just the most reliable streak mechanic on iOS. If Habitica's systems felt heavy, this is the reset.

### Finch

Finch is Habitica's gentler sibling: a self-care pet bird that grows as you complete goals, with journeys, mood tracking, and breathing exercises. Core features are free (and staying free, per the developers); Finch Plus runs around $9.99/month or $69.99/year. There are no HP penalties; skip a day and nothing dies. That makes it a better fit for mental-health-adjacent habits than for people who need a firm kick.

### Sweatcoin

Sweatcoin pays for steps: verified outdoor walking earns Sweatcoins you spend on vouchers and marketplace items. The free tier caps earnings at 10 coins per day; Premium (around $4.99/month or $29.99/year, region-dependent, with a 7-day trial) doubles the cap. It's a step counter with a reward loop, not a training tracker: great for daily walking motivation, useless for gym sessions.

## Moving your habit-RPG workflow into a fitness tracker

You don't have to abandon the system you've built. Most of Habitica's mechanics map cleanly onto a fitness tracker:

- **Dailies become your training plan.** Instead of a "workout" checkbox, you get scheduled sessions with actual exercises, sets, and rep targets; the game rewards completion, the data tracks progress.
- **XP and gold become levels and streaks.** Same dopamine loop, earned per set rather than per checkbox.
- **HP damage becomes streak breaks.** Missing a day costs you the chain instead of health points, with no double punishment for the same miss.
- **Party quests become guilds and leaderboards.** Group goals and friendly competition replace boss battles, with the same "don't let the team down" pull.
- **Gear and pets become cosmetics and achievements.** Still there if you like dressing up your progress.

If life-admin tasks (chores, bills, reading) still matter, run both: Habitica keeps the general habits, the fitness tracker owns training. For the wider tracker field, see our [workout tracker app alternatives](/workout-tracker-app-alternatives) guide.

## Which should you pick?

- **Stay on Habitica** if your fitness goals are general ("move more, sleep better") and the RPG loop is already working; nothing here beats it for pure habit RPG.
- **Run both** if you want the party system for life admin but need real workout logging.
- **Switch to Workout Quest** if training consistency is the actual goal; it's the only app in this list whose game layer reads real workout data.
- **Pick the specialist** if your bottleneck is specific: running motivation (Zombies, Run!), phone distraction (Forest), minimal chains (Streaks), gentler self-care (Finch), or step rewards (Sweatcoin).

Habitica is still the best habit RPG around; it's just not a workout tracker.
