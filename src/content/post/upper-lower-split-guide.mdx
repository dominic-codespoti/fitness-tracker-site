If you want a simple schedule that builds real muscle without living at the gym, the upper/lower split is hard to beat. It divides your training into upper-body days and lower-body days, and in its classic four-day form it hits every major muscle group twice a week. That frequency is exactly what the research favors, as we covered in our breakdown of [training muscle groups twice a week and the science behind it](/training-muscle-groups-twice-a-week-science). Below is the complete guide: the workouts, the sets and reps, and the 2-, 3-, and 4-day variants you can scale up or down.

## What Is the Upper/Lower Split?

An upper/lower split alternates one session for your upper body (chest, back, shoulders, arms) with one session for your lower body (quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves). Unlike a body-part split that trains chest on Monday and back on Tuesday, every workout here is a full half of your body.

The appeal is twofold:

- **Frequency without fatigue.** Each muscle gets hit twice weekly, which supports both strength and size, but each individual session stays manageable.
- **Four days, full coverage.** You get compound movements, enough volume, and still have three recovery days.

The classic schedule runs Upper A, Lower A, Upper B, Lower B. The "A" and "B" sessions are slightly different so your body gets variation while keeping the same movement patterns.

## The Classic 4-Day Upper/Lower Schedule

A typical week looks like this:

| Day | Session | Focus |
|-----|---------|-------|
| Monday | Upper A | Strength focus: heavy compounds |
| Tuesday | Lower A | Strength focus: squat pattern |
| Wednesday | Rest | Recovery |
| Thursday | Upper B | Hypertrophy focus: more volume |
| Friday | Lower B | Hypertrophy focus: hinge pattern |
| Saturday | Rest | Active recovery |
| Sunday | Rest | Recovery |

If your week doesn't line up with Monday through Friday, run the pattern in any four-day block — the important part is the alternating order, not the calendar.

### Upper A (Strength)

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
|----------|------|------|
| Barbell Bench Press | 4 | 5-6 |
| Bent-Over Barbell Row | 4 | 5-6 |
| Overhead Press | 3 | 6-8 |
| Pull-Ups | 3 | 6-8 |
| Incline Dumbbell Press | 3 | 8-10 |
| Barbell Curl | 2 | 8-10 |
| Triceps Pushdown | 2 | 8-10 |

### Lower A (Strength)

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
|----------|------|------|
| Back Squat | 4 | 5-6 |
| Romanian Deadlift | 3 | 6-8 |
| Leg Press | 3 | 8-10 |
| Leg Curl | 3 | 8-10 |
| Standing Calf Raise | 3 | 10-12 |
| Hanging Leg Raise | 3 | 12-15 |

### Upper B (Hypertrophy)

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
|----------|------|------|
| Incline Dumbbell Press | 4 | 8-10 |
| Seated Cable Row | 4 | 8-10 |
| Dumbbell Shoulder Press | 3 | 8-12 |
| Lateral Raise | 3 | 12-15 |
| Face Pull | 3 | 12-15 |
| Hammer Curl | 3 | 10-12 |
| Overhead Triceps Extension | 3 | 10-12 |

### Lower B (Hypertrophy)

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
|----------|------|------|
| Deadlift or Trap-Bar Deadlift | 3 | 5-6 |
| Front Squat or Goblet Squat | 3 | 8-10 |
| Walking Lunge | 3 | 10-12 per leg |
| Leg Curl | 3 | 10-12 |
| Seated Calf Raise | 4 | 12-15 |
| Cable Crunch | 3 | 12-15 |

Aim for one to two reps in reserve on the strength days — leave a rep or two in the tank — and push closer to failure on the hypertrophy days.

## The 2-Day and 3-Day Variants

Not everyone has four training days available, and that's fine. The upper/lower split scales down cleanly:

- **2-day variant:** One Upper day and one Lower day per week. This is a solid maintenance and beginner option, especially for busy schedules — see our [best 2-day gym workout routine](/best-two-day-gym-workout-routine) for a full plan.
- **3-day variant:** Upper, Lower, then a third full-body or repeat-Upper session. Many lifters run Upper, Lower, Upper, or Upper, Lower, Full Body. Our [best 3-day gym workout routine](/best-three-day-gym-workout-routine) lays out the options.
- **4-day variant:** The classic Upper A / Lower A / Upper B / Lower B above — also the heart of our [best 4-day gym workout routine](/best-four-day-gym-workout-routine).
- **5- and 6-day extensions:** If you want more frequency, add a fifth lower day or push to six days with a Lower C slot. See the [best 5-day gym workout routine](/best-five-day-gym-workout-routine) and [best 6-day gym workout routine](/best-six-day-gym-workout-routine) for how those schedules handle volume and recovery.

## Who Should Use the Upper/Lower Split?

The upper/lower split suits most lifters, but it shines for a few specific groups:

- **Busy intermediates.** Four focused days deliver more per hour than scattered daily sessions.
- **Anyone chasing strength and size together.** Twice-a-week frequency lets you practice compounds often while still accumulating volume.
- **Lifters recovering from bro-splits.** If you're used to training a muscle once a week, moving to twice is usually a fast win.

It's less ideal if you can only train two days and want maximum hypertrophy per muscle — a [full-body approach](/best-two-day-gym-workout-routine) often beats a bare upper/lower for that goal — or if you have a specific weak point that needs extra isolation work.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Running Upper B identical to Upper A.** The variation is what drives progress; keep the A/B differences.
- **Cramming in extra exercises.** Four quality lifts per muscle beats eight mediocre ones. If a session runs past 75 minutes, trim the isolation work.
- **Skipping the deload.** After six to eight heavy weeks, schedule a lighter week — our guide on [when and how to take a deload week](/deload-week) explains the timing.
- **Not tracking.** The split only works if you add weight or reps over time. Log every set so you can see progress — Workout Quest's built-in tracker makes this easy.

## Conclusion

The upper/lower split is one of the most efficient ways to organize your training: simple on paper, demanding in practice, and backed by solid training-frequency science. Start with the four-day classic, scale down to three or two days when life gets busy, and keep the A/B variation honest. Track your sets and reps in an app so your next session always has a target, and remember that the best split is the one you can run consistently for months.
