They switch because their app changed: a price increase, a free tier that lost features, a paywall around the routine they've used for years, or logging that feels like a chore. The category is crowded enough for nearly every training style — and with care, your history can move with you. Here are seven of the strongest trackers in 2026: what they cost, what the free tiers include, and who each suits. Torn between the two most popular loggers? ## The 7 Compared in Detail

### Workout Quest

Workout Quest turns gym logging into an RPG. Every set earns XP, levels unlock quests, and streaks keep the loop going — the game layer exists to make showing up rewarding. It's free to start, with full workout logging, an exercise library, quests, XP, and streak tracking; an AI coach adds guidance, and the optional premium tier covers cosmetics and extras. Everything works offline, so a dead gym signal never ends a session. Quit trackers out of boredom before? Try this one. See how quests fit into a weekly plan in our guide to [turning lifts into quests](/gamify-your-progress-workout-quest-quests), or browse more [gamified fitness apps](/best-gamified-fitness-apps).

### Strong

Strong's reputation rests on speed: start a workout, tap through sets, done. The free tier gives you unlimited workout logging, a 450+ exercise library, a rest timer, a 1RM calculator, and full history — but caps saved routines at 3, which makes a 4- or 5-day split awkward without paying. Pro (around $4.99/mo or $29.99/yr, or a lifetime option) removes the cap and adds custom exercises, advanced analytics, and CSV export. History, PRs, and volume trends stay visible at a glance — enough analytics for most. If your weeks look the same and you want the least friction possible, Strong wins on pure logging velocity.

### Hevy

Hevy is the social logger, and for many people the Strong replacement: unlimited workout logging free, with 4 saved routines, 7 custom exercises, and 3 months of history charts. Pro (around $2.99/mo or $23.99/yr, plus a lifetime option) lifts every cap and adds advanced analytics. The free tier is widely praised as one of the most generous in the category. The standout feature is migration: Hevy imports Strong's CSV directly, so a multi-year log moves over in minutes. Sharing workouts with friends and following other lifters keeps the habit social — a real consistency lever for many users. Between that and its community features, it's the default pick for anyone leaving Strong.

### Jefit

Jefit is the veteran of the group: a 1,400+ exercise library with form animations, a routine builder, and support for Android, iOS, and the web. The free tier covers workout logging and custom routines, with ads; Elite (around $12.99/mo or $69.99/yr) removes ads and adds advanced analytics, video demonstrations, smartwatch support, premium workout plans, and detailed training reports. A web dashboard also makes it easy to plan splits at a desk or on a bigger screen. The interface feels busier than minimalist loggers, but for a huge exercise database, community routines, and total flexibility, Jefit still delivers after all these years.

### Fitbod

Fitbod is the pure AI pick: there is no permanent free tier, just a 7-day trial, then around $15.99/mo or $95.99/yr. In exchange, the algorithm builds every session around your goal, equipment, and recovery, adjusting weights, sets, and reps from your history and managing fatigue with a muscle heatmap. Walk in, get told exactly what to do, done. Goals change easily in-app, and the algorithm re-tunes your plan without you rebuilding anything. For lifters who hate programming — or train in a gym with unusual equipment — Fitbod is hard to beat; the subscription is the trade-off for most users.

### Boostcamp

Boostcamp is for lifters who don't want to design their own training. The free tier includes 11,000+ programs — Stronglifts 5x5, 5/3/1, GZCLP, and hundreds of coach-designed plans — plus a complete tracker with RPE/RIR logging and a plate calculator. Its tracker handles supersets and deloads, so running a program properly is straightforward. The library includes community uploads, and a custom program builder covers everything else. Pro (around $59.99/yr) adds exclusive coach-designed programs, a Strength Score, and per-muscle volume heatmaps. For program followers the value is exceptional: the best-known strength programs are free, and the tracker around them is genuinely good.

### Alpha Progression

Alpha Progression sits between logger and coach. The free tier covers workout tracking and plan creation; Pro (around $12.99/mo or $79.99/yr, with a 14-day trial) adds an algorithm-generated plan builder, weight and rep targets for every set, plate and warm-up calculators, RIR tracking, and deload planning. The plan generator factors in your equipment, experience, and schedule. Targets are recalculated from every logged session, so you rarely have to guess what to lift next. It's a solid middle ground if you want automated progression advice without handing over control of your training — and its deload tooling pairs well with a [deload week](/deload-week).

## Migrating Your Workout History

- **Export first.** Most of these apps export history as CSV or JSON — though Strong's is a Pro feature, so do it before your subscription lapses.
- **The easy path: Strong to Hevy.** Hevy has a built-in importer for Strong's CSV. Set Strong to English and metric units before exporting, and expect to rebuild saved routines — the export contains history, not templates.
- **Everything else is manual.** Generic CSV import is rare; plan 15-30 minutes to rebuild routines.

## Which App Should You Pick?

- **Powerlifter or program follower:** Boostcamp — 5/3/1, GZCLP, and Stronglifts 5x5 free.
- **Beginner:** Workout Quest (free, guided, offline) or Jefit's big exercise library.
- **Gamification lover:** Workout Quest. If motivation is your bottleneck, an RPG layer beats a spreadsheet.
- **AI coaching:** Fitbod for equipment-aware sessions, Alpha Progression for plans plus control.
- **Social:** Hevy's community features are the strongest here.
- **Budget-conscious:** Workout Quest and Hevy have the most usable free tiers — see our [best free workout apps](/best-free-workout-apps) roundup.

## Bottom Line

No tracker is worth paying for if it makes you dread logging. Match the tool to the job: Boostcamp for programs, Fitbod or Alpha Progression for AI guidance, Hevy for social logging and easy migration, Workout Quest for consistency.
