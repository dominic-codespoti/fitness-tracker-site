"Free" is the most abused word in the fitness app store. Many apps that advertise a free version are really a 7-day trial with a paywall logo in the corner. Others start free and quietly shrink: MyFitnessPal's barcode scanner went Premium, Strava has pulled features into its subscription since 2024, and Sworkit's once-generous free tier now unlocks almost nothing for adults. But genuinely useful free apps do exist — you just have to know where to look, and what "free" means in practice.

This guide covers ten workout apps with real free tiers in 2026: what each includes, what is paywalled, and roughly what premium costs. New to app-based training? Start with our overview of [how mobile fitness apps make training more accessible](/how-mobile-apps-provide-accessible-fitness-experience) first.

## What "Free" Actually Means

Before the list, a reality check on how free tiers are structured:

- **Freemium (honest):** Core features — workouts, tracking, basic plans — are free forever; premium is optional. Nike Training Club is the purest example: no subscription exists at all.
- **Freemium (aggressive):** Free version nags, locks basic features, or hides your data behind a paywall. Read the fine print before committing years of history.
- **Trial masquerading as free:** "Free download" with a forced subscription after days. Skip these unless you're sure you'll pay.

Every app below has a genuinely usable free tier, though a few will upsell hard. Two things worth knowing: free apps still make money through ads, premium upsells, and data; and free tiers change over time, so treat "free forever" as marketing, not a contract. That's why data export matters most.

## What to Look For in a Free Workout App

Before you install anything, run it against this checklist:

- **Core logging or workouts are actually free.** If a "free" app paywalls every useful feature, walk away.
- **No forced trial.** A subscription countdown on day one is a trial, not a free tier.
- **Your history isn't held hostage.** Can you export or back up your data? If not, you're renting your progress.
- **Ads are tolerable.** Banners are fine; interstitials between sets are not. Check recent reviews.
- **Offline behavior.** If you train in a basement or travel, confirm logging works offline.
- **The free tier has survived a few years.** Apps that kept a real free tier through 2023-2026 are likely to keep one.
- **Privacy you can stomach.** For health data, check whether it's shared with advertisers before logging anything.

## The 10 Best Free Workout Apps in 2026

List prices as of mid-2026; confirm in the store listing before subscribing.

### 1. Workout Quest

Workout Quest is a free gym tracker that turns your training into a game. Log your sets, reps, and weights, and you earn XP, level up your character, and unlock quests as you build a streak. The gamification layer keeps you coming back, which is the whole battle. It's genuinely free, with optional premium cosmetics — nothing that affects logging is paywalled. For more on the gamified approach, see our roundup of [the best gamified fitness apps](/best-gamified-fitness-apps).

**Free tier:** Full workout logging, exercise library, quests and XP, streak tracking. No forced paywall. **Premium:** optional cosmetics and extras.

### 2. Nike Training Club

Nike made its premium training library free in 2020 and hasn't changed that: hundreds of guided workouts — strength, HIIT, mobility, yoga — led by real coaches, plus training programs and warm-ups, all ad-free. There's no paid tier at all, which makes NTC the easiest recommendation on this list.[^1]

**Free tier:** Nearly everything. **Paywalled:** nothing. VOD varies by country; most classes need a connection.

### 3. FitOn

FitOn offers celebrity-trainer workouts across strength, cardio, Pilates, and yoga, plus meditations. The free tier includes the entire workout library — the full class catalog, not a sampler.[^2]

**Free tier:** Unlimited workout and meditation library, trainer-led classes, progress tracking. Ads are present but tolerable. **Paywalled (FitOn Pro, around $30/year in most regions):** structured multi-week programs, offline downloads, personalized meal plans, premium music, and casting to TV.

### 4. JEFIT

JEFIT is a classic gym logger and routine builder. The free tier includes a database of over 1,400 exercises with instructions and animations, workout logging, and custom routine creation — enough to train with for years. It's ad-supported, and free accounts carry caps on saved routines and history depth.[^3]

**Free tier:** Logging, routine builder, 1,400+ exercise database, community. **Paywalled (JEFIT Elite, $12.99/month or $69.99/year):** advanced analytics and charts, professionally designed plans, HD exercise video, smartwatch support, no ads.

### 5. Seven – 7 Minute Workout

Seven is built around short, science-inspired 7-minute sessions you can do anywhere, no equipment. Gamified streaks and achievements keep you consistent, and the free tier is genuinely free forever — but it's the most limited here: one daily workout and basic tracking.[^4]

**Free tier:** Daily 7-minute workout, basic tracking, achievements. **Paywalled (7 Club, roughly $5-10/month or $40-80/year by region):** the full exercise library, custom plans, and trainer support.

### 6. Sworkit

Sworkit generates workouts from your available time and equipment. It used to have a solid free tier; it no longer does. As of 2026 the free version offers little beyond kids' content, and most workouts sit behind a subscription (7-day trial available). If you're an adult looking for free sessions, this isn't the app — it's listed so you know what you're getting when search results suggest it.[^5]

**Free tier:** Minimal — mostly kids' content. **Paywalled (roughly $60-110/year depending on plan and region):** almost the entire workout library, custom plan builder, offline access.

### 7. Strava

Strava is the standard for runners, cyclists, and outdoor athletes. The free tier still covers the essentials: unlimited GPS recording, basic stats, the social feed, and top-10 segment results. Full leaderboards, advanced analytics (Fitness & Freshness), offline maps, Beacon, and structured training plans are subscriber features now.[^6]

**Free tier:** GPS tracking, social feed, basic stats, free bulk data export. **Paywalled (Subscriber, $79.99/year or $11.99/month in the US):** advanced analytics, full segment leaderboards, offline maps, Beacon, training plans, and recaps like Year in Sport.

### 8. MyFitnessPal

Strictly speaking this is a nutrition app, but logging food is half the battle. The free tier still includes the enormous food database plus calorie and macro logging. One correction to common advice: the barcode scanner is no longer free — it moved behind Premium in late 2022 with custom macros and ad-free logging.[^7]

**Free tier:** Food logging, macro and calorie tracking, huge food database. **Paywalled (Premium, $79.99/year or $19.99/month in the US; Premium+ $99.99/year adds a meal planner):** barcode scanner, meal scanning, custom macros, ad-free logging, meal planner, recipes.

### 9. adidas Running (formerly Adidas Training)

The standalone Adidas Training app has been phased out; its programs now live inside the [adidas Running app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.runtastic.android), free to download, tracking 90+ sports and activities with basic stats, challenges, and structured plans from 5K to marathon. Advanced analytics sit behind the premium membership.[^8]

**Free tier:** GPS tracking for 90+ activities, basic stats, training plans. **Paywalled (premium membership):** advanced analytics and personalized coaching features.

### 10. Leap Fitness Home Workout

A simple, ad-supported app with no-equipment home workouts for abs, arms, legs, and full body, plus voice guidance. The entire workout library is free; you pay only to remove ads, via a one-time purchase or subscription depending on region and version.[^8]

**Free tier:** The full workout library with ads. **Paywalled:** ad removal (one-time or subscription, roughly $10/month or $30-70/year depending on region).

## How to Choose the Right One

The best free workout app depends on what you actually do:

- **Lifting weights at a gym:** Workout Quest or JEFIT for logging, progression, and routines. Torn between the two most popular minimalist loggers? Our [Hevy vs Strong comparison](/hevy-vs-strong) covers the paid-tier tradeoffs.
- **Guided classes at home:** Nike Training Club or FitOn.
- **Running or cycling outdoors:** Strava or adidas Running.
- **Short sessions, no equipment:** Seven or Leap Fitness Home Workout.
- **Want to fix your diet too:** MyFitnessPal alongside whatever you pick.
- **New to training:** Nike Training Club (guided), FitOn (variety), or Workout Quest (streaks).

For beginners, avoid data-heavy loggers: pick one app whose free tier covers your first month, use it consistently, and upgrade only when a specific feature becomes worth paying for. Before you commit years of history to anything, check that you can export it — our guide to [workout tracker app alternatives](/workout-tracker-app-alternatives) covers switching paths if you ever outgrow an app.

One common trap is installing five apps and using none of them consistently. Pick one primary tracker, stick with it for a month, and add a second only when the first becomes a habit. Consistency beats features every time — that's why apps that make showing up rewarding, like [gamified trackers](/best-gamified-fitness-apps), tend to outperform fancier ones in the long run.

## FAQ

### Are free workout apps really free?

Yes, for the core experience — with three caveats. Some free tiers are trials in disguise (set a reminder to cancel). Some make you the product, funding the app through ads and data sharing. And some shrink: MyFitnessPal's scanner, Strava's training features, and Sworkit's library all moved behind paywalls recently. Truly free apps — Nike Training Club above all — are rare.

### Do free workout apps sell my data?

"Sale" is a legal term: the honest answer is that it depends on the app and the law you're measuring against. MyFitnessPal says it doesn't expressly sell personal information, but it shares data with advertising partners for targeted ads — activity that counts as a "sale" or "sharing" under California's CCPA.[^7] Consumer fitness apps aren't covered by HIPAA either, so your data gets less legal protection than medical records. Read the privacy policy and use whatever opt-out controls exist before logging sensitive data. Apps funded purely by subscriptions have fewer reasons to share data.

### Can I export my workout data?

It varies — and it's the question nobody asks before starting. Strava is the gold standard: free bulk export of your full history — original GPX/FIT/TCX files plus a CSV summary — from account settings.[^6] Many paid loggers gate CSV export behind premium; some free apps offer none at all. Check before you build up years of history — if you can't leave, you're not really free.

### Which free app is best for beginners?

Nike Training Club: there's no subscription to misjudge and the guided programs make every decision for you. FitOn for variety, Workout Quest if you respond to streaks — beginners often quit in weeks 2-6, exactly the window gamification targets. For simple bodyweight training, Leap Fitness is the most straightforward.

### Do these apps work offline?

Loggers like Workout Quest, JEFIT, and Seven's daily session generally work offline because your history lives on the device. Guided video apps are the opposite: Nike Training Club streams classes, and FitOn gates offline downloads behind its Pro tier. If you train somewhere with poor reception, check the store listing for offline support first.

### Will free tiers keep shrinking?

The trend is real: free tiers are marketing tools, and since 2022 apps have locked features as they grow (MyFitnessPal's scanner, Strava's recaps, Sworkit's library). What survives are free tiers that still do the core job — logging or basic guided workouts — with ads or upsells on top. That's the filter this list uses and why Workout Quest and JEFIT are safer long-term bets.
