"Free" is the most abused word in the fitness app store. Many apps that advertise a free version are really a 7-day trial with a paywall logo in the corner. But genuinely useful free apps do exist — you just have to know where to look. This guide covers ten workout apps with real free tiers that actually work, what each one includes, and where the limits are. If you're new to app-based training, start with our overview of [how mobile fitness apps make training more accessible](/how-mobile-apps-provide-accessible-fitness-experience) before you decide what to download.

## What "Free" Actually Means

Before the list, a reality check on how free tiers are structured:

- **Freemium (honest):** The core features — workouts, tracking, basic plans — are free forever. Premium exists but isn't required to get value.
- **Freemium (aggressive):** Free version exists but nags constantly, locks basic features, or hides your data behind a paywall.
- **Trial masquerading as free:** "Free download" with a forced subscription after days. Skip these.

The apps below are all in the first category. Every one of them is usable for weeks or months without paying a cent — though a few will try hard to upsell you.

## The 10 Best Free Workout Apps in 2026

### 1. Workout Quest

Workout Quest is a free gym tracker that turns your training into a game. Log your sets, reps, and weights, and you earn XP, level up your character, and unlock quests as you build a streak. The gamification layer keeps you coming back, which is the whole battle. It's genuinely free to start, with optional premium cosmetics and features. For more on the gamified approach, see our roundup of [the best gamified fitness apps](/best-gamified-fitness-apps).

**Free tier:** Full workout logging, exercise library, quests and XP, streak tracking. No forced paywall.

### 2. Nike Training Club

Nike made its premium training library free in 2020, and it's stayed that way. You get hundreds of guided workouts — strength, HIIT, mobility, yoga — led by real coaches, plus training programs and warm-ups.

**Free tier:** Nearly everything. No subscription required at all. Ad-free, too.

### 3. FitOn

FitOn offers celebrity-trainer workouts across strength, cardio, Pilates, and yoga, all free with an optional premium tier for personalized plans and offline downloads.

**Free tier:** Unlimited workout library, trainer-led classes, progress tracking. Ads are present but tolerable.

### 4. JEFIT

JEFIT is a classic for gym-goers who want a serious workout logger and routine builder. You can browse thousands of exercises with form animations and build your own splits.

**Free tier:** Workout logging, routine builder, exercise database. Premium adds analytics and custom charts.

### 5. Seven – 7 Minute Workout

Seven is built around short, science-inspired 7-minute sessions you can do anywhere with no equipment. The gamified streaks and achievements make it easy to stay consistent.

**Free tier:** Daily 7-minute workout, basic tracking, achievements. Premium unlocks the full exercise library and custom plans.

### 6. Sworkit

Sworkit generates workouts from your available time and equipment — no equipment, gym, or a mix. It's popular with beginners and travelers because you can get a session in 5 to 60 minutes.

**Free tier:** Time-based workouts with a limited but solid exercise library. Premium unlocks all exercises and custom plans.

### 7. Strava

Strava is the standard for runners, cyclists, and anyone tracking outdoor activity. The free tier covers GPS tracking, stats, segments, and a huge community.

**Free tier:** Activity tracking, segment leaderboards, social feed. Premium adds advanced analytics and training plans.

### 8. MyFitnessPal

Strictly speaking this is a nutrition app, but it belongs on any free-fitness list because logging your food is half the battle. The barcode scanner and enormous food database are free.

**Free tier:** Food logging, barcode scanning, calorie and macro tracking. Premium adds more granular insights.

### 9. Adidas Training

Formerly Runtastic Results, Adidas Training offers structured bodyweight and equipment programs with video guidance and adaptive difficulty.

**Free tier:** A rotating set of programs and workouts with ads. Premium unlocks the full library and custom plans.

### 10. Leap Fitness Home Workout

A simple, ad-supported app with no-equipment home workouts for abs, arms, legs, and full body, with voice guidance and beginner-friendly pacing.

**Free tier:** The full workout library with ads. A one-time payment removes ads; no subscription required.

## How to Choose the Right One

The best free workout app depends on what you actually do:

- **Lifting weights at a gym:** Workout Quest or JEFIT for logging, progression, and routines.
- **Guided classes at home:** Nike Training Club or FitOn.
- **Running or cycling outdoors:** Strava.
- **Short sessions, no equipment:** Seven or Leap Fitness Home Workout.
- **Tight on time and want variety:** Sworkit.
- **Want to fix your diet too:** MyFitnessPal alongside whatever you pick.

One common trap is installing five apps and using none of them consistently. Pick one primary tracker, stick with it for a month, and add a second only when the first becomes a habit. Consistency beats features every time — that's why apps that make showing up rewarding, like [gamified trackers](/best-gamified-fitness-apps), tend to outperform fancier ones in the long run.
