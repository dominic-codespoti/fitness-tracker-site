Hevy and Workout Quest solve two different problems that both look like "tracking your workouts." Hevy is a polished minimalist set-and-rep logger with a strong social layer. Workout Quest is a full tracking app wrapped in a game: XP, quests, streaks, and an AI coach whose entire job is keeping you consistent. If you're comparing them, you're really choosing between a better logbook and more motivation.

This guide compares pricing, free tiers, logging depth, gamification, AI features, platforms, and data export so you can pick the right fit. Torn between Hevy and another minimalist logger instead? Our [Hevy vs Strong comparison](/hevy-vs-strong) covers that matchup.

## Quick verdict

**Hevy is the better pure logger.** If you already train consistently, follow a written program, and just want fast, distraction-free set tracking with friends cheering you on, Hevy is hard to beat — its free tier is genuinely usable and Pro costs less than most subscriptions in the category.[^1]

**Workout Quest is the better motivator.** If your actual bottleneck is showing up — skipped sessions, abandoned programs, weeks off — Workout Quest attacks that problem directly. Every logged set earns XP, streaks and quests reward consistency, and an AI coach adapts your plan when life gets in the way.

The honest note up front: neither is a bad app, and they aren't strict substitutes. For most lifters, consistency — not logging speed — is what limits progress. If that sounds like you, read on.

## The case for Hevy

### A genuinely generous free tier

Hevy's free version covers unlimited workout logging with caps that only bite power users: 4 saved routines, 7 custom exercises, and 3 months of visible history.[^2] Most lifters running a standard 3- or 4-day split can use it indefinitely without paying. Upgrading to Pro ($2.99/month, $23.99/year, or a one-time $74.99 lifetime purchase)[^1] removes those caps and adds all-time statistics, a warm-up calculator, and deeper muscle-distribution charts.

### Social accountability and clean design

Where Hevy shines is the combination of frictionless logging and a real social layer: follow friends, see their sessions in your feed, trade likes and comments. For lifters motivated by peers rather than points, that's a legitimate retention tool — the same psychological lever behind [gamified fitness apps](/best-gamified-fitness-apps), minus the game dressing. Native Apple Watch and Wear OS apps round out a mature, well-built package.

The trade-offs: Hevy deliberately has no deep programming layer. There's no adaptive AI coach rewriting your plan around missed sessions or limited equipment, and beyond likes and comments there's nothing rewarding the habit of showing up itself. If motivation isn't your problem, none of that matters.

## The case for Workout Quest

### Gamification aimed squarely at consistency

Workout Quest treats adherence as the product. Logging a session earns XP, individual muscles level up as you train them, streaks and quests reward repeated visits, and loot chests and avatar cosmetics give you something tangible to unlock. Guilds, co-op quests, raid-boss workouts, and leaderboards add a team dimension Hevy's feed doesn't attempt. None of it replaces good programming — but it directly targets the failure point where most programs die: week three.

There's a reason we keep coming back to this theme across the blog: tracking drives progressive overload, and progressive overload only works if you [keep training consistently over months](/mastering-muscle-growth-the-science-of-tracking-for-progressive-overload). A tracker that makes showing up feel rewarding is doing the job that matters.

### An AI coach that adapts, not just records

Underneath the game layer sits a serious tracker: a 1,000+ exercise library with GIF demonstrations, offline mode, Apple Health and Apple Watch sync, and CSV/PDF/GPX export so your history stays yours. The AI coach builds and adjusts programs around your training history, RPE feedback, and available equipment, with an AI fitness chat for questions between sessions. Advanced fatigue analytics and readiness signals from sleep and HRV tell you when to push and when to back off. Macro tracking with a barcode scanner lives in the premium tier, covering the nutrition side Hevy leaves entirely alone.

The trade-offs are the mirror image of Hevy's: more going on screen, and if you find game elements distracting mid-set, Hevy's restraint will suit you better.

## Which should you pick?

- **Pick Hevy** if you already train consistently, want the fastest possible logging with a friendly social feed, value native Wear OS support, or want a cheap lifetime purchase on a proven minimalist logger.
- **Pick Workout Quest** if staying consistent is your struggle — if you've quit programs before, skip sessions when motivation dips, or want a coach that adapts your plan instead of a blank logbook. It's also the stronger pick if you want nutrition tracking and recovery analytics in the same app.
- **Use both**, even: some lifters run Workout Quest as their primary tracker for the coaching and quest loop while keeping Hevy installed for quick partner sessions. It costs nothing to try either free tier.
