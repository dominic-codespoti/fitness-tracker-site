Push pull legs, or PPL, is one of the most popular training splits in any gym: one day for pushing movements, one for pulling, one for legs. It scales from three days a week to six, and every major muscle group gets direct work. This guide covers the classic 3-day schedule, the 4-day hybrid, and the 6-day version, with day-by-day tables, sets and reps, progression, and common mistakes.

## What Is PPL and Why Does It Work?

PPL organizes training by movement pattern rather than by individual muscle:

- **Push day:** chest, shoulders, triceps. Bench press, overhead press, lateral raises, triceps extensions.
- **Pull day:** back, biceps, rear delts. Rows, pull-ups, face pulls, curls.
- **Legs day:** quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves. Squats, hinges, lunges, calf raises.

The logic: every muscle gets dedicated volume, and because the days train different patterns, a muscle that works hard on Monday has the week to recover.

The frequency math matters. In the classic three-day PPL, each muscle trains about once a week, which works but isn't what science favors for growth. At six days, every muscle gets hit twice weekly, the sweet spot covered in [training muscle groups twice a week: the science](/training-muscle-groups-twice-a-week-science). ## The Classic 3-Day PPL

The classic 3-day PPL spreads the three sessions across the week with a rest day between each:

| Day | Session | Muscle Groups |
|-----|---------|---------------|
| Monday | Push | Chest, shoulders, triceps |
| Wednesday | Pull | Back, biceps, rear delts |
| Friday | Legs | Quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves |

If that doesn't fit, run any push-pull-legs order with rest days in between.

### Day 1: Push

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
|----------|------|------|
| Barbell Bench Press | 4 | 6-8 |
| Incline Dumbbell Press | 3 | 8-10 |
| Overhead Press | 3 | 6-8 |
| Lateral Raise | 3 | 12-15 |
| Triceps Pushdown | 3 | 10-12 |

### Day 2: Pull

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
|----------|------|------|
| Pull-Ups or Lat Pulldown | 4 | 6-8 |
| Bent-Over Barbell Row | 4 | 6-8 |
| Seated Cable Row | 3 | 8-10 |
| Face Pull | 3 | 12-15 |
| Barbell Curl | 3 | 8-10 |

### Day 3: Legs

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
|----------|------|------|
| Back Squat | 4 | 6-8 |
| Romanian Deadlift | 3 | 8-10 |
| Leg Press | 3 | 10-12 |
| Leg Curl | 3 | 10-12 |
| Standing Calf Raise | 4 | 12-15 |

That's roughly 16 to 18 working sets per session, with two to three minutes of rest on compounds, one to two on isolation, a rep or two in the tank. It's also the setup we recommend in our [best 3-day gym workout routine](/best-three-day-gym-workout-routine).

## The 4-Day PPL + Upper Hybrid

Four days a week? The most popular upgrade adds one upper-body day to the PPL sessions:

| Day | Session | Notes |
|-----|---------|-------|
| Monday | Push | Same as 3-day version |
| Tuesday | Pull | Same as 3-day version |
| Thursday | Legs | Same as 3-day version |
| Friday | Upper | Second upper hit of the week |

Rest on the other three days.

### Friday: Upper

| Exercise | Sets | Reps |
|----------|------|------|
| Incline Dumbbell Press | 3 | 8-10 |
| Seated Cable Row | 3 | 8-10 |
| Overhead Press | 3 | 8-10 |
| Pull-Ups | 3 | 6-10 |
| Lateral Raise | 3 | 12-15 |
| Barbell Curl | 3 | 10-12 |
| Triceps Pushdown | 3 | 10-12 |

The extra upper day pushes chest, back, shoulders, and arms to twice-weekly frequency; legs stay at once. If legs are the priority, swap it for a second leg session, or use a straight [upper/lower split](/upper-lower-split-guide), which trains everything twice a week in four days. ## The 6-Day PPL

The six-day version runs PPL twice: Push A, Pull A, Legs A, Push B, Pull B, Legs B, one rest day. Every muscle trains twice a week, with different exercises and rep ranges per A/B pair.

| Day | Session | Focus |
|-----|---------|-------|
| Monday | Push A | Bench 4x5-6, overhead press 3x6-8 |
| Tuesday | Pull A | Rows or deadlift 4x5-6, pull-ups 4x6-8 |
| Wednesday | Legs A | Squat 4x5-6, RDL 3x6-8 |
| Thursday | Push B | Incline press 4x8-10, lateral raises 3x12-15 |
| Friday | Pull B | Cable rows 4x8-10, curls 3x10-12 |
| Saturday | Legs B | Front squat 3x8-10, leg press 3x10-12, calves 4x12-15 |
| Sunday | Rest | Recovery |

Six days demands real recovery: sleep and food must back it up, and per-session volume should stay moderate. ## How to Progress: Double Progression

The split doesn't build muscle by itself; make each week harder, and the cleanest way is double progression:

1. Pick a rep range for each exercise (the tables above do).
2. Add reps within that range each week, from the bottom toward the top.
3. Hit the top with good form, add the smallest weight increment, and drop back to the bottom.

If your range is 6-8, the goal is 8 clean reps; once there, add weight and start at 6. Log every set, weight, reps, and how hard it felt, so the next session has a target. Workout Quest makes this easy: log sets, earn XP, and watch your strength curve climb.

## Who Should Use PPL (and Who Shouldn't)

PPL suits lifters who like structure and can commit to three or more gym days a week. Its compound-first selection builds a balanced physique if you don't skip pulling and leg work.

It's not for everyone:

- **Complete beginners** often do better with full-body training, which practices every muscle more often with less complexity. Our [best 2-day gym workout routine](/best-two-day-gym-workout-routine) is a gentler start.
- **Two-day lifters** can't fit a full PPL rotation; an upper/lower or full-body plan gets more done.
- **Anyone with a lagging muscle group** should watch the volume balance: PPL gives equal attention by design, so weak-point work goes on top.

## Common PPL Mistakes

- **Too much pressing, too little pulling.** Three pressing moves for every pulling one is a fast route to shoulder problems. Balance them with rows and face pulls.
- **Treating leg day as squat-only.** Hamstrings need their own work; Romanian deadlifts and leg curls aren't optional.
- **Adding volume instead of progressing.** More exercises isn't progression; adding weight or reps is. Double progression beats a longer workout.
- **Running six days without the recovery to back it up.** If sleep and food don't support it, sessions get worse; four harder days beat six mediocre ones.
- **Not tracking.** PPL only works if you know what you did last week. Log your sets and reps; a tracker automates it.

## Conclusion

The push pull legs routine earns its popularity: simple structure, full muscle coverage, and workable schedules from three to six days. Start with the classic 3-day version, add an upper day for more frequency, and progress with double progression. Most of all, pick the version you can sustain; consistency beats any split on paper.
