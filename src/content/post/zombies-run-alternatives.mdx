## Why runners outgrow Zombies Run

Zombies, Run! — the audio-drama running game from Six to Start, co-created by novelist Naomi Alderman — is still the reference point for narrative-driven cardio. You're a Runner in the apocalypse: GPS tracks your real pace while missions play out as an audio story, zombie chases speed up when you slow down, and the supplies you collect build Abel Township between runs.

Its current state is worth knowing before you commit. As of mid-2026 it's available on iOS and Android and follows a freemium model: free to download, with episodes unlocking weekly, then a subscription for the full library — around $6.99/month, $49.99/year, or $89.99/year for VIP (which adds virtual races and perks). Prices can shift, and long-time users may hold grandfathered "Legacy" rates — check in-app. The catalog spans 600+ missions, plus Zombies Run 5K Training and The Walk, and one subscription carries across platforms via your ZombieLink account.

So why do people leave? Stories end, the monthly fee starts to feel heavy for a game you've already finished, and the loop only gamifies one side of fitness. If your runs have outgrown the apocalypse — or you want competition, data, coaching, indoor options, or game layers on your strength work — one of these will fit. For a wider view of the category, our [best gamified fitness apps](/best-gamified-fitness-apps) roundup covers the whole field.

## The alternatives, app by app

### Workout Quest

Workout Quest is the gym-side complement to running apps rather than another runner: it gamifies strength training. Every set earns XP, levels unlock quests, and streaks keep the loop alive — the same RPG machinery as Zombies Run, aimed at barbells instead of base building. It's free to start, with full workout logging, an exercise library, quests, XP, and streak tracking, and an optional premium tier on top. Everything works offline, so a dead gym signal never ends a session. For runners who lift, that's the point: you can log strength work beside your runs without a second subscription. See how quests fit into a weekly plan in our [guide to turning lifts into quests](/gamify-your-progress-workout-quest-quests), or compare it with the [other workout trackers](/workout-tracker-app-alternatives) if you'd rather keep logging separate.

### Walkr

Walkr, from Fourdesire (now part of the SPARKFUL brand), turns your phone's step counter into a spaceship. Every step generates energy that fuels exploration of a galaxy with 100+ planets, each with its own population and resource-management minigame; you join fleets and complete "epics" with friends. It's free on iOS and Android with in-app purchases (an ad-free "Turbo" mode and convenience items). It's not a run tracker — no GPS pace, no audio drama, no intervals — so it works best as ambient daily motivation alongside a serious run app. Best if your Zombies Run habit was really about keeping yourself moving daily.

### Strava

Strava's gamification is quieter but addictive: every public route becomes a segment, and segments are leaderboards where you race your past self and nearby strangers. Monthly challenges award badges, clubs add social accountability, and the free tier already covers run tracking, segments, and challenges. Premium (around $11.99/month, region-dependent) adds live segments, relative effort, and training plans. It won't tell a story or level up an avatar, but for competitive runners the data loop is its own game — the free tier alone is one of the strongest in running.

### Nike Run Club

Nike Run Club is the coaching option: completely free, with guided runs from Nike coaches, audio-guided speed runs, and recovery runs, plus challenges and achievements that keep streaks going. There's no subscription and no paywall — the easiest recommendation for runners who want structure without cost. Where Zombies Run distracts you from the workout with a story, NRC leans into the workout itself — coach cues, pace targets, and encouragement. Best for beginners building a habit and anyone who wants a free, well-produced training companion.

### Zwift Run

Zwift brings its virtual cycling world to running: you appear as an avatar in a digital city, join group runs and races, and follow structured workouts — while your real pace drives the avatar on screen. As of 2026, running on Zwift is free (the ~$19.99/month subscription applies to cycling; run-only access doesn't need it), though you'll want a treadmill with a foot pod, a smart treadmill, or a compatible shoe sensor. For indoor runners who dread the treadmill, Zwift's races and group events supply the social and competitive fix outdoor running usually provides.

### Ring Fit Adventure

Ring Fit Adventure isn't a running app at all — it's a full console RPG on the Nintendo Switch that uses exercise to fight battles. Squats, presses, and yoga poses, detected through the Ring-Con and leg strap, are your attacks; you level up, earn skill points, and progress through a fantasy campaign. You pay once (around $79.99 at retail) with no subscription. It won't improve your 5K, but when weather or schedule keeps you inside, it turns living-room movement into a game — and its strength work pairs well with a running week.

## Pairing a running app with a gym tracker

Most runners don't only run. If you're doing strength work on top of your mileage, the clean setup is two tools: a running app for the road, and a dedicated gym tracker for the gym — each playing its own game. That's where Workout Quest slots in: it's free, works offline, and gives your squats and presses the same quest-and-XP treatment Zombies Run gives your miles, so the motivation loop doesn't break when you switch from road to rack. Recovery is part of the deal too — plan [active recovery](/active-recovery) days between hard runs and lifts. If you wear a smartwatch, its activity games (Apple's Activity rings, Fitbit's daily goals and challenges) can bridge both sides and keep daily movement honest — see our [guide to wearables and recovery quests](/smart-recovery-quests-wearables) for wiring them into a training week.

## Which app should you pick?

- **You still love the story:** finish the remaining missions — or re-engage before switching.
- **Daily movement, not pace:** Walkr gamifies steps, not splits.
- **Competition and social proof:** Strava's segments and challenges.
- **Free structured coaching:** Nike Run Club.
- **Treadmill time:** Zwift Run's virtual races.
- **Living-room sessions:** Ring Fit Adventure.
- **Hybrid training:** keep your run app for the road, add Workout Quest for the gym.

## Bottom line

Zombies Run turned running into a game, and the genre it started has plenty of room for what it can't do: data, competition, indoor worlds, coaching, and a game layer for your strength work. Pick the loop that matches the training you actually do — and if that training includes the gym, [try Workout Quest on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/workout-quest-fitness-tracker/id6452191825) alongside your run app.
