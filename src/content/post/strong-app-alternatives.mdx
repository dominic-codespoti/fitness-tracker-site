Strong is one of the most loved workout trackers ever made, and for good reason: it logs sets, reps, and weights faster than almost anything else, its Apple Watch app is superb, and it has quietly done its job for over a decade. But "loved" doesn't mean "right for everyone," and plenty of long-time users eventually go looking for something more.

This guide covers five real alternatives — what each does better than Strong, what it costs, and who it actually fits. If you're torn between just two options, our [Hevy vs Strong comparison](/hevy-vs-strong) goes deeper on the head-to-head details.

## Why people leave Strong

Strong's minimalism is the whole appeal — and also why people outgrow it. The common reasons:

- **Analytics sit behind PRO.** Progress charts, volume tracking, 1RM trends, and body measurements are locked behind Strong PRO, along with CSV export of your own history.[^1]
- **The free tier caps you at 3 saved routines.** Fine for full-body or push/pull/legs, restrictive once you run upper/lower splits, 5-day programs, or rotate templates.[^1]
- **No native Wear OS support.** Android watch owners have no first-party option.[^2]
- **No social or motivation layer.** No feed, friends, streaks, or anything that pulls you back on a low-energy day.
- **No programming guidance.** Strong assumes you already know what you're doing. If you want an app to tell you what to lift next, it won't.

None of these are flaws if you want a pure logger. They're gaps — and every app below fills at least one of them.

## The five alternatives

### Hevy: Strong, but modern and social

If you like how Strong feels but want more around it, Hevy is the closest match. The logging flow is nearly identical, but the free tier lets you log unlimited workouts (with caps on saved routines, custom exercises, and history depth),[^3] there's a social feed where friends can like and comment on sessions, and native Wear OS support means Android watch owners finally get wrist logging. Pro costs less than Strong PRO ($23.99/year vs $29.99) and includes a cheaper lifetime option ($74.99), plus official Strong CSV import so your migration is mostly painless.[^2]

**Pick Hevy if** you want Strong's speed plus friends, a better free tier, and a Wear OS watch. Our full [Hevy vs Strong](/hevy-vs-strong) breakdown covers the trade-offs, including how to move your history.

### Workout Quest: when the problem is motivation, not features

Some people leave Strong not because it lacks charts but because nothing in it makes them want to open it. Workout Quest attacks that directly: every set earns XP, your character levels up, muscles level individually, and quests, streaks, loot chests, and guilds turn showing up into the game itself. Underneath the game layer is a serious tracker: a 1,000+ exercise library with GIF demos, rest timers, a muscle heat map, and an AI coach that builds programs from your history, effort (RPE), and available equipment. It's free to start on iOS and Android, works offline, syncs with Apple Health and Apple Watch, and exports your data as CSV, PDF, or GPX. If gamification sounds gimmicky to you, it's worth knowing the psychology is the same lever Hevy's social feed pulls — we explain it in our guide to [gamified fitness alternatives](/habitica-alternatives-fitness).

**Pick Workout Quest if** you skip gym days more often than you'd like and want an app that rewards consistency, not just logging.

### Boostcamp: stop writing programs, follow proven ones

Strong gives you a blank page. Boostcamp hands you the library: nSuns 5/3/1, GZCLP, 5/3/1 variations, and 130+ coach-designed programs from names like Eric Helms and Alberto Nuñez, all free with no ads, alongside a capable tracker with RPE logging and a plate calculator.[^4] The optional Pro tier (~$59.99/year) adds analytics like strength scores and per-muscle volume heatmaps plus exclusive programs — but the program library itself stays free.[^4]

**Pick Boostcamp if** your real gap is programming guidance and you'd rather follow a proven plan than build your own.

### JEFIT: maximum depth for data people

JEFIT has been around even longer than Strong and goes deeper on structure: an exercise database of over 1,400 movements with instructions and animations, a full routine builder, and detailed progress charts and analytics.[^5] The free tier is genuinely usable (ad-supported), while Elite unlocks advanced stats, professionally designed plans, HD exercise videos, and smartwatch support at $12.99/month or $69.99/year.[^5]

**Pick JEFIT if** you want the deepest feature set in the category and don't mind a busier interface than Strong's clean lines.

### Fitbod: hand the decisions entirely to the algorithm

Fitbod takes automation further than any app here: tell it your goal, equipment, and how recovered each muscle group is, and it generates every session for you, adjusting weights and exercises as you go. There's no permanent free tier though — new users get limited free workouts before hitting a paywall, then it's $15.99/month or $95.99/year.[^6]

**Pick Fitbod if** you'll happily pay to never think about programming again, and you don't need a social layer.

## Which should you pick?

- **Stay on Strong** if pure fast logging is all you want — none of these beat it at that, and switching apps has real costs.
- **Pick Hevy** if you want the closest experience to Strong with social features, a stronger free tier, and Wear OS support.
- **Pick Workout Quest** if consistency is your bottleneck and a game layer on top of real training data would keep you coming back.
- **Pick Boostcamp** if you need a program to follow more than a fancier tracker.
- **Pick JEFIT** if you want depth: huge exercise database, detailed analytics, total routine control.
- **Pick Fitbod** if you want an algorithm to run your training completely, budget permitting.

Whichever way you lean, check our roundup of the [best free workout apps](/best-free-workout-apps) before paying for anything — several of these do their core job for free.

Strong earned its reputation as the fastest logger in the store, and if it still serves you, keep it.
